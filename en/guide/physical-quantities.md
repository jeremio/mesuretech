# Physical quantities

## Quantity or unit?

A **physical quantity** is a measurable property of a phenomenon, a body or a substance: the length of a table, the mass of an apple, the temperature of a room. It exists regardless of how it is expressed.

A **unit** is the reference chosen to express it: the meter, the kilogram, the degree Celsius. A measurement is always written as **number × unit**: "2 m" and "200 cm" describe the same length.

::: tip Key takeaway
- The quantity says **what** is measured (a length, a mass, a duration).
- The unit says **with what** it is measured (the meter, the kilogram, the second).
- Converting means changing the unit without changing the quantity.
:::

## The seven base quantities

The International System (SI) rests on seven quantities considered independent of each other. All others are derived from them. Each has a **dimension symbol**, used in dimensional analysis.

| Quantity | Dimension | SI unit | What it describes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length | L | meter (m) | Distance between two points |
| Mass | M | kilogram (kg) | Amount of matter in the sense of inertia: a body's resistance to acceleration |
| Time | T | second (s) | Duration of a phenomenon |
| Electric current | I | ampere (A) | Flow rate of electric charge |
| Thermodynamic temperature | Θ | kelvin (K) | Level of thermal agitation of particles |
| Amount of substance | N | mole (mol) | Number of elementary entities (atoms, molecules…) |
| Luminous intensity | J | candela (cd) | Light power emitted in one direction, per unit solid angle, weighted by the sensitivity of the human eye |

## Derived quantities

Other quantities are defined from the base quantities. Their dimension is written as a product of base dimensions.

| Quantity | Definition | SI unit | Dimension |
|---|---|---|---|
| Area | length × length | square meter (m²) | L² |
| Volume | length³ | cubic meter (m³) | L³ |
| Speed | distance ÷ duration | meter per second (m/s) | L·T⁻¹ |
| Acceleration | speed ÷ duration | m/s² | L·T⁻² |
| Force | mass × acceleration | newton (N = kg·m/s²) | M·L·T⁻² |
| Pressure | force ÷ area | pascal (Pa = N/m²) | M·L⁻¹·T⁻² |
| Energy | force × distance | joule (J = N·m) | M·L²·T⁻² |
| Power | energy ÷ duration | watt (W = J/s) | M·L²·T⁻³ |
| Frequency | 1 ÷ period | hertz (Hz = s⁻¹) | T⁻¹ |
| Plane angle | arc length ÷ radius | radian (rad) | dimensionless |

## What dimensions are for

Only quantities of the **same dimension** can be added or compared. This is a simple way to check a calculation or a formula:

- a speed multiplied by a duration gives L·T⁻¹ × T = L, a length indeed;
- adding meters and seconds makes no sense;
- a conversion never changes the dimension: feet convert to meters, never to kilograms.

::: info Dimensionless quantities
Some quantities are ratios of two quantities of the same dimension: an angle (length ÷ length), a percentage, a refractive index. They have no dimension, but may still have a unit, such as the radian or the degree.
:::
